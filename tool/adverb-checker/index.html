What is an adverb checker?

An adverb checker reads your text and marks the words that act as adverbs. This adverb checker is built for a fast pass over a sentence or a short paragraph. It lists the adverbs it finds, counts them and shows their share of the total words. Because everything happens in your browser, the text never leaves the page.

The point is not to replace a grammar book. It is to give you a quick read on where the modifiers are, so you can spot a string of ly words and decide which ones earn their place.

How the adverb checker works

Two rules do the work. The first is the ly suffix test: a word ending in ly is usually an adverb, as in quickly or carefully. The second is a short list of common irregular adverbs that do not end in ly, including very, quite, well, fast, never, always and often. An exception list removes false hits such as only, family and reply, which end in ly but are not adverbs.

This is a heuristic, a practical rule rather than a full grammar parse. It is fast and it works for the ordinary vocabulary of emails, essays and captions. Words outside that vocabulary are where it becomes less certain.

How to identify an adverb by hand

Paste your text and the strip under the box fills with the words the tool found. If you want to know how to identify adverbs yourself, ask what the word modifies. An adverb usually changes a verb (she ran quickly), an adjective (very good) or another adverb (quite slowly). If you remove it and the sentence keeps its core meaning, it was probably a modifier.

Learning how to identify an adverb this way makes the tool easier to judge. You will see when a listed word is right and when a rare adverb slipped past the rules.

Adverb types at a glance

Word typeExample
ly suffix adverbquickly, softly, carefully
Irregular adverbvery, quite, well
Frequency adverboften, always, never
Short form adverbfast, hard, late
Exception, not an adverbonly, family, reply
Exception, ly endinglonely, lovely, silly

The table shows why a word list alone is not enough. Some adverbs, such as fast and hard, carry no ly ending at all, while some ly words are adjectives. The checker handles both by pairing the suffix rule with exceptions.

What is the adverb in this sentence

Paste a single line to see what is the adverb in this sentence. The highlighted words are the exact matches the tool counted, and the unique figure tells you whether the same adverb appears twice. For what is the adverb in the sentence with a tricky word, check the result against a dictionary, because the rules cover common usage only.

Using adverb density to cut clutter

Density is the share of words in your text that the tool counts as adverbs. A paragraph where one word in six is an adverb is carrying a lot of modifiers. Cut the ones that repeat what the verb already says, and the sentence gets tighter. Try pasting two versions of the same paragraph to see which one reads cleaner.

When the adverb finder misses a word

No rule based tool is complete. Rare adverbs are missing from the small list, and an adverb inside a long clause may not match a simple suffix check. That is fine for a quick review of plain sentences and the reason the copy here is careful about what it claims. Use the adverb finder as a second pair of eyes, then make the final call yourself.

Adverbs are easy to add and hard to justify. A checker that lists them is really a prompt to delete half of them.
Questions

Adverb Checker FAQ

What is an adverb checker?

An adverb checker reads your text and lists the words it treats as adverbs. This free adverb tool uses an ly suffix rule and a short list of common irregular adverbs such as very and often. It also reports adverb density so you can see whether a paragraph is overloaded.

How do I identify adverbs in a sentence?

Paste your text and read the strip under the box. It shows the adverbs in this sentence that the tool found. If you want to know how to identify an adverb or how to identify adverbs in general, look for words that modify a verb, an adjective or another adverb.

What is the adverb in this sentence?

The highlighted list shows what is the adverb in this sentence as the tool sees it. For what is the adverb in the sentence with a more unusual word, remember the checker works from rules, not from a full parse, so verify against a dictionary.

Is this an adverb generator?

No. This is an adverb finder and checker, not an adverb generator. It finds adverbs in text you paste, it does not invent new ones. Use a thesaurus when you want to replace a plain adverb with a stronger word.

Why are only and family not counted?

They end in ly but are not adverbs. The checker keeps an exception list that includes only, family, reply, lonely and silly, so those words are skipped. That keeps the count honest for ordinary sentences.

Does it find every adverb?

Not every one. The method is a heuristic built on suffixes and a small word list, so rare adverbs and adverbs buried in complex clauses can be missed. It is best on short, plain sentences, and I would not rely on it for a formal grammar assignment.

How do I use adverb density?

Density is the share of words in your text that the tool counts as adverbs. A high number usually means the writing leans on modifiers, and cutting a few ly words makes sentences tighter. Compare two drafts by pasting each one in turn.
