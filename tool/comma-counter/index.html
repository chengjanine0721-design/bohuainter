What is a comma counter?

A comma counter reads a passage and reports how many commas it contains alongside the words and sentences around them. Paste text into the box above and the panel shows the comma count, the word count, the sentence count, the character total, the number of words per comma and the longest sentence. Together those figures describe how heavily a paragraph is punctuated.

This is not a grammar checker. It counts the marks that are there and leaves the judgement to you, which makes it useful for a second pass when a draft feels dense but you cannot see why.

How to count commas in a paragraph

Paste the paragraph exactly as written and read the figures above. To count commas by hand, run a finger along the line and tick one for every mark, but that method loses your place in a long block. The counter never does. It also reports words per comma, which turns a raw tally into a measure of how much text sits between pauses.

If you are revising, paste a version, note the numbers, edit and paste again. A paragraph that drops from one comma every eight words to one every fourteen has changed rhythm, and the change is easier to feel once you can see it.

When commas normally appear

ScenarioTypical use
Lists and seriesBetween three or more items
Introductory phrasesAfter an opening word, phrase or clause
AppositivesAround a phrase that renames a noun
Nonrestrictive clausesBefore a clause that adds extra information
Dates and addressesBetween the day and year, city and state
Joined independent clausesBefore a conjunction that links two full sentences

The table is a guide to where commas earn their place. Each one marks a small break that helps a reader group words. When a sentence has several of these at once, the comma count climbs and the line can become hard to follow.

What words per comma tells you

Words per comma is the average gap between marks. A low number means frequent pauses and a choppy or carefully measured line. A high number means long runs with little structure, which is where readers lose the thread. Neither is wrong on its own. The useful question is whether the gaps match the rhythm you intended.

Nonfiction written for a broad audience often settles near one comma every ten to fifteen words. Dialogue usually has fewer. Legal and academic prose often has more. Use the figure as a comparison against your own work rather than a target to hit.

The longest sentence and comma load

The longest sentence figure shows the word count of the heaviest line in your text. Read that sentence on its own. If it needs several commas to hold together, it is a candidate for splitting. A comma counter cannot make that decision, but it points at the exact line where the decision is worth making.

Short sentences carry less punctuation and read faster. Mixed with a few longer ones, they give a passage pace. A run of long comma heavy sentences tends to flatten that pace, which is why the longest sentence figure is the one editors check first.

When a comma count is not enough

A comma can be missing, misplaced or unnecessary, and a tally treats all three the same. Two commas around a single word may be correct, while a pair around a vital clause can change the meaning. The counter reports structure, so pair it with a read aloud before you publish anything that matters.

Because the count runs in the browser, you can test a single paragraph or a whole article without uploading a draft. Nothing is stored, so a piece you are still working on stays on your own device.

Questions

Comma Counter FAQ

What is a comma counter?

A comma counter tallies the commas in your text and sets them beside the word and sentence totals. This tool also gives you characters, words per comma and the word count of the longest sentence.

How do I count commas in my writing?

Paste the text into the box and read the comma count above. For a short line you can tick each mark by hand, but for a paragraph or a full article the counter is faster and does not lose your place.

What does words per comma mean?

It is the average number of words between commas. A low figure means frequent pauses and a measured line. A high figure means long stretches with few breaks, which can be hard to read.

Does the comma counter check my grammar?

No. It counts the marks that are present and leaves the judgement to you. Use it to find dense sentences and odd gaps, then read the passage aloud to decide whether each comma belongs.

How many commas is too many in one sentence?

There is no fixed rule. A sentence that needs four commas to stay clear may be fine, but it is often a sign that the idea is doing too much. Check the longest sentence figure and consider splitting anything that runs long.

Can the comma counter handle a whole article?

Yes. Paste as much text as you like. The count stays accurate across long documents, and nothing is uploaded or stored, so an unpublished draft remains on your device.

Is the comma count the same as the number of pauses?

No. A comma is one kind of pause and a full stop is another. The counter reads commas only, so use the sentence figure alongside it when you are thinking about the rhythm of a passage.
