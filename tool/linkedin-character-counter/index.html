What are the LinkedIn character limits?

LinkedIn splits its limits by field, and a professional profile uses six of them regularly. A post or update allows 3,000 characters, a comment 1,250, a headline 220, the About section 2,600, an article 110,000 and a connection note 300. Company taglines sit at 120 and job titles at 100.

Because the ceiling is generous, writers rarely hit it. The real constraint is the fold. A post is folded behind a see more link after roughly three lines, so the first 140 to 210 characters carry the entire decision to keep reading. The LinkedIn character counter above reports both numbers: the distance to the hard limit and the characters that survive the fold.

The fold is the part that decides whether a post works

Write the first two lines as a complete thought that earns the click. A number, a contradiction, a short admission or a result all work. A greeting does not. The pattern most teams settle on is a one line claim, a blank line, then the detail, because the blank line pushes the fold a little further down and makes the claim stand alone.

The chars before fold figure measures your opening paragraph, the text before the first line break. Keep it under about 140 characters and the whole hook is visible on a phone. Keep it under 210 and it survives the desktop feed.

How to use this LinkedIn character counter

It takes a few seconds:

  1. Choose the field. Post, comment, headline, About, connection note or article each load their own limit.
  2. Paste or type the text. Characters, words, lines, reading time and the hook length update live.
  3. Watch the bar. It turns amber when the count passes the limit, and the remaining figure goes negative.
  4. Copy the result back into LinkedIn once the numbers look right.

The About section: 2,600 characters that work harder than the headline

The headline is what search shows. The About section is what a person reads after deciding you are worth a second look. Its first two lines appear before the see more link, so treat them as a headline of their own: who you help, with what, and the proof that you have done it.

After that opening, three short paragraphs beat one long one. What you do now, what you did before that makes it credible, and how to reach you. The 2,600 character budget is enough for about 400 words, and almost nobody reads 400 words in a profile. Aim for 150 to 200 and leave the rest unused.

Comments, connection notes and articles

A comment of 1,250 characters is a short essay, and LinkedIn folds it in the feed long before that. The comments that get replies tend to be two sentences: one that adds a specific fact or a counter example, and one that asks something answerable. If the point needs more than 300 characters, it is probably a post.

Connection notes allow 300 characters and are read once, by one person, in a notification list. Say who you are, why this person specifically, and nothing else. Articles allow 110,000 characters, which is a book chapter scale. Length is not the constraint there, structure is: an article with three subheads and 1,200 words outperforms a 4,000 word wall every time.

LinkedIn limits in short

  • LinkedIn post character limit is 3,000 characters, including spaces and line breaks.
  • LinkedIn article character limit is 110,000 characters, which is roughly 18,000 words.
  • Character limit for LinkedIn posts and the limit for comments are two different numbers, 3,000 against 1,250.
A LinkedIn post is judged in the first two lines. Everything after that is for the people who already decided to read.
Questions

LinkedIn Character Counter FAQ

What is the character limit on a LinkedIn post?

3,000 characters, including spaces and line breaks. That is roughly 450 to 500 words, which is far more than most readers see because the feed folds the post after about three lines.

What is the LinkedIn About section character limit?

2,600 characters. It is the longest text field on a personal profile apart from articles, and the first two lines are the only part that shows before a see more link.

Is the LinkedIn comment limit different from the post limit?

Yes. A comment allows 1,250 characters, less than half a post. Long replies get truncated in the feed, which is one reason short comments with one clear point perform better.

How long can a LinkedIn article be?

110,000 characters, which is roughly 18,000 words. The practical limit is much lower: an article that takes more than eight minutes to read loses most of its audience long before the end.

What is the LinkedIn headline character limit?

220 characters. Only about 60 to 70 show in search results and connection requests, so the useful part of the headline has to arrive early.

Where does LinkedIn cut off a post with see more?

At roughly 210 characters on desktop and around 140 on a phone. The hook statistic above counts the characters before the first line break in your text, so you can see what a reader gets for free.

Does LinkedIn count hashtags and mentions in the character count?

Yes. A hashtag or a mention is just text to the counter, so a tag such as hashtagseo costs its full length. The words figure treats each tag as one word.
