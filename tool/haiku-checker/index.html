What makes a haiku a haiku

A haiku is a short poem in three lines with a 5-7-5 syllable pattern, seventeen syllables in total. It usually names a season, holds one clear image and stops before explaining itself. The shape is easy to describe and surprisingly hard to hit by ear, which is why a haiku checker is more useful than it sounds: most drafts are one syllable off, and the syllable that is wrong is rarely the one you suspect.

In Japanese the seventeen units are on, a sound length that does not map one to one onto English syllables. A strict 5-7-5 count in English is therefore a convention, widely used in classrooms and competitions, rather than a translation of the original rule. This checker measures English syllables, which is the convention most writers are judged against.

How the haiku checker works

Type each line into its box. The badge on the right shows the syllable count against the target for that line, so a first line of six syllables reads 6 / 5 in amber while a correct one reads 5 / 5 in green. The verdict line reports the whole poem once all three boxes have text, and the form field switches to 5-7-5 when the pattern holds.

The count comes from grouping vowels inside each word, dropping a silent final e and treating endings such as ed and es the way English speakers usually pronounce them. That handles the common vocabulary of poetry well. Proper nouns and unusual words are the places where any automatic counter, including this one, can disagree with your ear, so read those lines aloud.

How to count syllables in a haiku line

Say the line out loud and count the beats where the jaw drops. That physical test beats any rule. Then check the number against the badge. When the two disagree, the word is usually a compound or a word with a swallowed vowel, such as every, which most speakers say in two beats and some counters read as three.

Two habits shorten the work. Prefer short concrete words, because a haiku built from one and two syllable words is easy to count and easier to hear. And cut articles where the line allows it, since a and the cost a syllable each and rarely carry an image.

If you are writing for a class, follow the pattern your teacher asked for. If you are writing for a journal, read what the journal publishes, because many editors now accept haiku that break 5-7-5 while keeping the image and the seasonal turn.

Using the haiku maker as a starting point

The make one button assembles a complete 5-7-5 poem from a small word bank. Every line is verified before it appears, so the result is always in form. Treat it as scaffolding: keep the shape, replace the images with something you actually saw, and the poem becomes yours. A generated line about a pond is worth less than your own line about the particular puddle outside your door.

The same button is useful when you are stuck on the middle line. Haiku fail most often at line two, where the poem has to turn. A seven syllable line that introduces a second image, rather than restating the first, is what the middle line is for.

Is this a haiku? Three common mistakes

The first is counting words instead of syllables, which produces a poem that looks short and scans long. The second is explaining the image in the last line instead of letting it land, which turns seventeen syllables into a sentence with a moral attached. The third is ignoring the season, which is optional in modern English haiku but still the fastest way to signal that you know the tradition.

None of those mistakes is fatal. A haiku that is one syllable off but holds a real image will be remembered longer than a technically perfect poem about nothing. Fix the count last, after the image is right.

Questions

Haiku Checker FAQ

How many syllables are in a haiku?

Seventeen, split across three lines as 5, 7 and 5. In Japanese those units are on rather than English syllables, and the two do not line up exactly, which is why a strict 5-7-5 count in English is a convention rather than a law.

How do I check whether my poem is a haiku?

Paste each line into the boxes above. The badge next to every line shows its syllable count against the target, and the verdict line reports whether all three match. If they do, the poem is in haiku form.

How does this haiku syllable counter work?

It groups vowel sounds inside each word, drops a silent final e, and treats a final ed or es the way English speakers usually do. Common words land correctly, and unusual proper nouns are the cases worth checking by ear.

Can this tool write a haiku for me?

Yes. The make one button assembles a haiku from a small word bank, with every line verified at 5, 7 and 5 syllables. Use it as a starting point, then replace the words with your own image and keep the counts.

What is the difference between a haiku and a senryu?

Both use 5-7-5. A haiku refers to a season and to nature. A senryu uses the same shape for human behaviour, often with a wry or ironic turn. If your poem is about people rather than weather, it is a senryu.

Does a haiku need a seasonal word?

In the Japanese tradition, yes. A kigo, a word that signals the season, anchors the poem in time. English language haiku often keep this loosely, with a specific image doing the work that a kigo does in Japanese.

Is a 5-7-5 poem always a haiku?

No. Syllable count is the shape, not the content. A 5-7-5 poem about a spreadsheet is closer to a senryu or a short poem. The form is necessary and not sufficient.
