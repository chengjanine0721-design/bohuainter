What is a verb finder?

A verb finder reads text and pulls out the words that act as verbs. This verb finder runs in your browser and lists both action verbs and the auxiliaries that help them. It exists for the moments when you want to check a sentence quickly, without opening a grammar book or parsing the whole paragraph by hand.

The result is a list and a few counts, nothing more. That is deliberate. A tool that helps you identify verbs in a plain sentence is useful, and a tool that claims to understand every clause is usually wrong in quiet ways.

How the verb finder works

Three methods are combined. A built-in list covers common verbs such as run, write and build. An auxiliary list covers is, are, was, were, have, has, will and can. Finally, ing and ed endings catch forms such as running and finished that may not appear in the list. Together they handle the ordinary verbs of daily writing.

This is a heuristic rather than a full grammar parser. It is fast, it needs no network access, and it is honest about its edges. Rare verbs and unusual forms are where you should double-check the result.

How to find the verb in a sentence

Paste your text and the strip below the box fills with the matches. If you want to learn how to find a verb in the sentence yourself, look for the action first. In The dog barked, the action is barked. Then look for a helper such as is or has, because English verbs often come in pairs. To find the verb in a negative sentence, ignore the word not and look on either side of it.

Verb types at a glance

Word typeExample
Action verbrun, write, build
Auxiliary verbis, are, was, were
Modal verbwill, can, must
Have verbhave, has, had
ing formrunning, writing
ed formwalked, finished
Can be noun or verbwork, light

The last row is the awkward one. Words such as work and light change class with the sentence, and no suffix rule settles that on its own. The verb finder takes its best guess from the word list, which is why a quick review still matters.

What are the verbs in this sentence

Paste one line to see what are the verbs in this sentence. Each match appears in the strip, and the counters show the total, the unique verbs and how many auxiliaries were present. A line such as She is running returns both is and running, which is correct, because English often builds a verb from more than one word.

To be verbs and auxiliaries

Forms of be are the most common helpers in English. A to be verb finder would look for am, is, are, was and were, and those words are already in the auxiliary list here. They carry tense and agreement even when the main action is elsewhere, so they are worth counting on their own. The auxiliaries figure in the panel tells you how many were found.

When the verb finder can miss a verb

The word list is small on purpose, so a rare or technical verb may not appear in it. An irregular past tense can slip past the ed rule. A noun that looks like a verb, such as work in hard work, can be counted by mistake. These are the normal limits of a heuristic, and they are why this page describes itself as a quick verb finder rather than a complete parser. Paste one sentence at a time for the clearest answer.

The verb carries the sentence. Find it first, and the rest of the grammar usually falls into place.
Questions

Verb Finder FAQ

What is a verb finder?

A verb finder reads your text and lists the words it treats as verbs. This verb finder uses a built-in list of common verbs, a set of auxiliaries such as is, are and have, and simple ing and ed endings. It is a quick helper to identify verbs in plain sentences rather than a full grammar checker.

How do I find the verb in a sentence?

Paste your text and read the list below the box. To find the verb in a sentence by hand, look for the action or the state of being. If you want to know how to find a verb in the sentence with more than one clause, check each clause separately.

What are the verbs in this sentence?

The highlighted list shows what are the verbs in this sentence as the tool reads it, including any auxiliaries it spotted. A sentence such as She is running has both is and running, and both appear in the count.

Is this a subject and verb finder?

No. This is a verb finder, not a subject and verb finder. It locates verbs and auxiliaries, but it does not identify the subject. Find the subject by asking who or what performs the action.

What is a to be verb finder?

A to be verb finder looks for forms of be, such as am, is, are, was and were. Those words are included in the auxiliary list here, so a sentence with a linking verb still returns a result. They matter because they carry tense without an obvious action.

Does it catch every verb?

Not always. The method is a heuristic built on a word list and endings, so irregular or rare verbs can be missed and a noun like work can be mistaken for a verb. Treat this verb checker as a sentence verb finder for quick drafts, not as a strict verb locator for formal work.

Can I paste a long paragraph?

You can, but accuracy falls as clauses pile up. The per sentence figure gives an average, and the auxiliaries count shows how many helping verbs were found. For careful work, paste one sentence at a time.
