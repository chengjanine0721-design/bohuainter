What a website word counter actually measures

A website word counter answers one question: how much readable text is on this page. Paste the page and you get words, unique words, characters, paragraphs and reading time, plus a running match count for any word you are looking for. Everything runs in your browser, so a client page or a draft under embargo never leaves your machine.

The unique words figure is the one most people ignore and later need. A page of 1,200 words built from 300 distinct words repeats itself heavily, and repetition is what a reader feels as padding even when the word count looks healthy. If unique words sit below a third of total words, the page is saying the same thing in different clothes.

How to count words on a web page in three steps

  1. Copy the page. Open it in a browser, press Ctrl+A then Ctrl+C. Selecting inside the article instead of the whole page gives a cleaner number.
  2. Paste it above. If you copied from the page source or a CMS, the HTML is stripped before counting.
  3. Read the figures. Words for length, unique words for variety, paragraphs for scannability and reading time for how long a visitor will actually spend.

For a competitor page, this is the fastest way to see whether a ranking article is genuinely more thorough or just longer. Compare words per section rather than the total, because a 3,000 word page with twelve sections is not deeper than a 1,500 word page with six.

Does word count still matter for SEO?

Word count is not a ranking factor. Coverage is. The reason longer pages tend to rank is that they usually answer more of the questions a searcher brings, and search engines reward pages that resolve the query rather than pages that pad to a target. A 700 word page that answers the question and the three obvious follow ups will beat a 2,000 word page that circles it.

Where the count does help is diagnosis. If a page underperforms and sits at 400 words against competitors at 1,500, it is probably missing sections rather than keywords. If it underperforms at 2,500 words with a high unique word count, length is not the problem and adding more will not fix it. The keyword density list above is the quickest check on the second case: five different words all clustering around the same idea usually means the page repeats one section instead of building three.

How many words should a page have

These ranges come from what actually ranks across content types. Treat them as a sanity check, not a quota.

Page typeTypical length
Home page400 to 800 words
Blog post1,000 to 2,000 words
Pillar guide3,000 to 6,000 words
Product page300 to 600 words
Category page200 to 500 words
Landing page500 to 1,000 words
FAQ answer40 to 80 words

A page far below the range for its type is usually thin. A page far above it is usually two pages wearing one URL, which is a stronger argument for splitting than for trimming.

How to search words on a web page

Ctrl+F on Windows or Command+F on a Mac opens the browser find bar and reports how many times a term appears. It is the fastest way to check whether a page mentions a specific product, a competitor, a price or a claim. The find box above does the same job on pasted text, with the advantage that the count survives editing: change a sentence and the match total updates immediately.

Both routes match substrings, so searching for ship also finds shipping and relationship. For a precise check, search the longer form. When you are auditing your own page, the useful question is not how often a keyword appears but whether it appears in the places that matter: the title, the first paragraph, at least one subhead and the closing section.

Why thin pages get ignored

A page with 200 words and no specific detail gives a search engine nothing to distinguish it from the ten thousand pages saying the same thing. Thin content is not punished by a word count rule. It is outranked by pages that answer more, and it is abandoned by readers who arrive, scan for the answer and leave.

The fix is rarely to add words. It is to add one thing the page does not have: a table, a worked example, a comparison, a number with a source, a screenshot of the actual interface. A 500 word page with a good table beats a 1,800 word page of generalities, and it takes less time to write.

A word count tells you how much you wrote. Unique words and paragraphs tell you whether anyone will read it.
Questions

Website Word Counter FAQ

How do I count the words on a website?

Open the page, press Ctrl+A to select everything and Ctrl+C to copy, then paste it into the box above. The counter strips menus, scripts and tags if you paste HTML, and the words figure is the readable text only.

Does this tool crawl a URL for me?

No. It counts what you paste, which keeps the tool free, instant and private. If you need an automated crawl, use a paid SEO tool. For a single page check, copying the page takes about five seconds.

How many words should a page have for SEO?

Long enough to answer the query completely and no longer. Comparison pages often land between 1,500 and 2,500 words, product pages between 300 and 600. Word count is a symptom of depth, not a ranking factor on its own.

What counts as thin content?

A page that leaves the main question unanswered. Under about 300 words of real text, most pages cannot cover a topic with any specificity, which is why thin pages and low word counts so often appear together.

How do I search for a specific word on a web page?

Press Ctrl+F on Windows or Command+F on a Mac and type the word. The browser shows how many matches exist and jumps between them. The find box above does the same for pasted text and keeps a running total.

Why is the word count different from what an SEO tool reports?

Tools disagree about what to include. Some count navigation and footer text, some strip it, some count numbers and hyphenated compounds differently. This counter measures the text you pasted and nothing else.

Does HTML markup affect the count?

No. If the pasted text looks like HTML, the tool removes script and style blocks, strips the tags and decodes entities before counting, so you get the word count a reader would see.
