What is a Chinese word counter?

A Chinese word counter does a job English counters cannot: it reads text with no spaces between words and still gives you a number you can act on. Paste a paragraph above and this Chinese word counter reports eight figures at once, from the pure hanzi count to sentences and reading time, and every figure updates on each keystroke. Nothing is uploaded, because all counting runs inside your browser.

The headline number uses the convention your editor already trusts. Microsoft Word counts each Chinese character as one word and each English word as one word, and this tool follows the same rule. When someone asks for a word count Chinese standards apply to, that is the figure they expect to see.

Chinese characters vs words: why the two numbers differ

Chinese is written without spaces, so a word count in Chinese is always an estimate built by grouping characters. A single character such as 人 is a word on its own, while two or three characters combine into units like 电脑 or 电脑游戏. Linguistic research puts the average Chinese word at roughly 1.5 to 2 characters, so 500 hanzi usually works out to somewhere between 250 and 330 words. That gap is exactly why a Chinese character word count report shows both numbers instead of pretending one number answers every question.

Which number should you care about? For schoolwork, publishing and most official requirements in China, the answer is the character count, usually written as 字数. For translation quotes, the industry bills by the thousand characters. For bilingual documents, the Chinese word count that mixes hanzi plus English words is the closest match to what Word displays.

How to use this Chinese character count tool

It takes a few seconds:

  1. Paste or type your Chinese, English or mixed text into the box. Counts update live as you write.
  2. Read the Chinese characters figure for the hanzi total, the number most Chinese requirements are written against.
  3. Check the word count figure when a brief is written in words rather than 字数, for example a 300 word abstract for an English journal.
  4. Use punctuation, sentence and paragraph counts to sanity check structure before you submit or publish.

The sample button loads a short mixed language paragraph so you can see how each box reacts before pasting your own text.

Counting mixed Chinese and English text

Modern Chinese text rarely stays pure. Product names, citations and technical terms arrive in English, and a good Chinese character counter has to keep the two streams honest. This tool counts hanzi one by one, counts English words as whitespace separated tokens, and keeps the breakdown visible: English words sit in their own box, punctuation from both scripts sits in another, and the main word count is simply the sum of hanzi and English words. Numbers count as English words, and hyphenated terms such as state-of-the-art count as one.

One hanzi is one word, one English word is one word. That single rule makes your Chinese word count match Word, and Word is the number people check.

Chinese Word Counter FAQ

How many words are in Chinese text?

Chinese has no spaces between words, so a word count is an estimate. Most research puts the average Chinese word at 1.5 to 2 characters, so 500 Chinese characters is roughly 250 to 330 words. This Chinese word counter uses the same convention as Microsoft Word: each hanzi counts as one word, and each English word counts as one.

Is a Chinese word count the same as a character count?

No. The Chinese character count is the number of hanzi in the text, while the Chinese word count groups characters into words. For schoolwork and most requirements in China, the character count is what is actually checked, which is why both numbers are shown here.

How does this Chinese character counter handle mixed Chinese and English text?

It reports the two parts separately. Chinese characters are counted one by one, English words are counted as whitespace separated tokens, and the word count figure is the sum of both, which matches how Word reports 字数 for bilingual documents.

Does the count include punctuation?

Punctuation is counted in its own box rather than being added to the hanzi total, so you always see the pure Chinese character count separately from full stop and comma marks. The characters figure excludes spaces.

Is the word count the same as Microsoft Word?

Yes for the main figures. Word counts each Chinese character as one word and each English word as one word, and this Chinese word counter follows the same rule, so the number you see here matches what a teacher or editor sees in Word.

Is this Chinese character count tool free?

Yes. It is free with no signup and no limits, and all counting runs inside your browser. Your text is never uploaded or stored.
