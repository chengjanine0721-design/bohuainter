How Many Words Is a Typical Novel

Genres differ more than most guides admit. Here are the real ranges for debut and established novels, by category, with page counts to match.

A typical novel runs 80,000 to 100,000 words for adult fiction. That single range covers most of what agents and editors expect, and it is the figure to aim for when you have no other information about the market. The range is not fixed, though. Genre moves it by tens of thousands of words in either direction, and a first novel faces a tighter ceiling than the fifth book from the same author.

The short answer, and why it is a range

Publishers buy manuscripts that fit a shelf. A book far outside its genre's convention is harder to place, not because the writing is worse but because both the production cost and the reader expectation change with length. An 85,000 word thriller sits comfortably on the shelf. A 160,000 word thriller from an unknown writer reads to a buyer as an expensive risk.

So the answer to how many words is a typical novel has two parts. For adult fiction in the common genres, plan on 80,000 to 100,000 words. Then adjust for genre, for your publication history and for the specific publishers you are submitting to.

The table gives the range agents and editors generally accept, plus the figure most authors land near. Treat the middle column as the outer limit rather than a target, and the right column as a safe place to aim.

Why fantasy and science fiction run long

The genre needs room for a world before it needs room for a plot. Maps, magic systems, invented history and the reader's gradual orientation all consume pages. Readers of the category actively want length, which is why a 150,000 word epic fantasy can sell while a 150,000 word thriller gets rejected on length alone.

Science fiction sits slightly lower because it tends to carry one big idea rather than a whole invented culture. A 100,000 word space opera is normal. A 100,000 word near future novel about a single technology is already generous.

The debut penalty

A first novel faces a harder word count ceiling than an established one. Publishers take a risk on an unknown writer, and a very long manuscript costs more to edit, print and launch. An agent who reads 80,000 words and loves them can picture the book in shops. An agent staring at 240,000 words from an unknown name sees an editing bill and a printing bill.

The practical ceiling for a debut in most adult genres is around 100,000 to 110,000 words. Above that, structural cuts usually make the book stronger anyway. Once you have a readership, the ceiling lifts, which is why the fourth book in a series is often the longest.

Romance, crime and thriller, shorter by design

These categories reward pace. Romance readers read quickly and often, so a 70,000 word novel that can be finished in a weekend fits the habit better than a 130,000 word one. Crime and thriller follow the same logic, with the added constraint that suspense decays if the middle sags, and a long middle is the easiest place for it to sag.

Series crime is the exception. Once a detective has a following, 90,000 to 100,000 words becomes ordinary, because readers are buying time with a character rather than a single puzzle.

Young adult and middle grade

Young adult novels usually land between 55,000 and 80,000 words. Fantasy in the category is allowed more, sometimes past 100,000, because the same world building appetite applies. Middle grade sits between 30,000 and 55,000 words, and the lower end is not a compromise. A 35,000 word middle grade novel is a complete book, not a short one.

Below middle grade, chapter books run from a few thousand words to about 15,000, and picture books are measured in hundreds of words rather than thousands. Word count stops being the organising principle at that age and page count takes over.

Novellas, short novels and very long books

A novella has no formal definition. In practice it means 20,000 to 40,000 words, a length that publishers rarely print alone and often bundle into a collection. Digital publishing has made the form viable again, since there is no print run to justify.

At the other end, books over 150,000 words exist in every genre, and they are almost always either established authors or self published. If you are querying, a very long manuscript is a marketing problem before it is an artistic one.

How word count turns into pages

A standard manuscript page, double spaced in 12 point type, holds about 250 words. That puts an 80,000 word novel at roughly 320 manuscript pages and a 120,000 word novel at 480. A printed paperback page holds 250 to 300 words, so the printed book will be close to the same figure.

If a submission asks for a page count rather than a word count, convert with the manuscript standard, not with the paperback. The words to pages calculator does both, single and double spaced, for US Letter and A4.

How to measure a draft you already have

Word processors disagree slightly on what counts as a word, mostly around hyphenated compounds, numbers and ellipses. The difference is a few hundred words in a full manuscript, which does not change anything. What does change things is knowing your count early enough to plan.

Paste the draft into the character counter to see words, characters, sentences and paragraphs together in one panel. If you track chapters, the paragraph counter and the sentence counter show whether your blocks and sentences are drifting long as the book grows.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many words is a typical novel?

For adult fiction, 80,000 to 100,000 words. Literary fiction and fantasy stretch higher, romance and crime sit lower, and a debut is usually held to the lower half of its genre range.

Is 50,000 words enough for a novel?

It counts as a novel by most award definitions, which set the floor at 40,000 words. Commercially it is short for adult fiction, though it fits romance, young adult and middle grade, and it is normal for a novella or a digital first release.

How many words is too long for a novel?

Above 120,000 words you need either a genre that expects length, such as epic fantasy, or a readership that already knows your name. For a debut in a mainstream genre, anything past 110,000 words invites requests to cut before the writing is even discussed.

Does word count matter to literary agents?

Yes, as a signal before they read a page. Agents know what their editors can sell, and a manuscript far outside the range tells them the writer has not studied the market. A count inside the range removes one reason to say no.

How many words should a first chapter be?

Between 2,000 and 5,000 words, with 3,000 as a common target. The number matters less than what the chapter does, but a 12,000 word opening chapter is a pacing warning that agents will notice.

Conclusion

Plan for 80,000 to 100,000 words, then move inside that band according to what you are writing. Fantasy and science fiction can go to 150,000 and still be conventional. Romance, crime and thriller are better at 70,000 to 90,000. Young adult wants 55,000 to 80,000 and middle grade 30,000 to 55,000. If this is your first book, stay under 110,000 no matter what genre you write, because the debut ceiling is real and it is enforced by economics rather than taste.

Once the draft exists, count it before you send it anywhere. A quick pass through the character counter tells you the total, and comparing that total against your genre's range is the difference between a submission that looks considered and one that looks unread.
